# First aid for minor burns (Riverside Community Clinic, fictional protocol RCC-04)

> FICTIONAL TRAINING SAMPLE. Written for a programming course. NOT medical advice. Do not use for real patients.

## What counts as a minor burn here
A burn smaller than the patient's palm, not on the face, hands, feet, genitals or over a joint, and not caused by electricity or chemicals. All other burns are referred (RCC-05).

## Immediate care
1. Cool the burn under cool (not ice-cold) running water for 20 minutes. Cooling helps even if the burn happened up to 3 hours ago.
2. Remove rings, watches and tight clothing near the burn before it swells. Do not pull off clothing that is stuck to the skin.
3. Do not put butter, oil, toothpaste or ice on a burn.
4. Do not break blisters.
5. Cover loosely with a clean non-stick dressing or clean plastic film.

## Pain and follow-up
Give pain medicine only as written on the clinic dosing chart. Ask the patient to return in 2 days, or earlier if the burn shows signs of infection (spreading redness, pus, fever).

## Children
Burns in young children need extra attention: check that the story of how the burn happened makes sense, and tell the nurse in charge if you are worried about the child's safety.
