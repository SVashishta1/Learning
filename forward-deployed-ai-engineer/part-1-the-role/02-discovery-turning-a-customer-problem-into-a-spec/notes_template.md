# Discovery notes: <customer> · <date>

> Lines that start with ">" are hints. Replace them with what you heard. Write "TBD" for anything you still need to find out.
> Write numbers whenever you can ("about 40%", "6 minutes per ticket"). Quote people word for word when it matters.

## Who we talked to
> Name, role, team, and how they relate to the problem (sponsor, user, IT, security...).

## The problem in their words
> One or two direct quotes. Then one sentence in your own words.

## Current process (step by step)
> What happens today, from trigger to finish. Who does each step, in which tool, how long it takes.

## Pain and impact (numbers)
> Time, money, errors, customer complaints, staff turnover. Where did each number come from?

## Users and volume
> Who would use the solution, how many of them, how often, how many items per day/week.

## Data sources
> Where the information lives (databases, files, emails, people's heads). Format, size, quality, who owns it.

## Systems and integrations
> Tools involved, how we can reach them (API, database, export, nothing), who can give access.

## Security, privacy and compliance
> Personal data, regulations (GDPR, HIPAA...), data residency, SSO, approvals needed, where AI may run.

## Success looks like
> How they will judge success. Turn feelings into a number you could measure, and write today's value (baseline).

## Constraints (time, budget, people)
> Deadlines, budget, who on their side has time to help, change freezes, holidays.

## Stakeholders and decision makers
> Who pays, who decides, who can block, who will use it daily. Who is enthusiastic and who is worried.

## Risks and unknowns
> What could make this fail. What we don't know yet.

## Next steps
> Who does what by when. Include the follow-up questions.
