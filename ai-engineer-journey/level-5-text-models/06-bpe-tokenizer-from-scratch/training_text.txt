The tokenizer is the very first step of every large language model. Before a model can read a sentence, the sentence must be turned into numbers. The simplest way is to give every character a number, but then sentences become very long. Another way is to give every word a number, but then the vocabulary becomes huge and the model cannot handle new words. Byte pair encoding sits in the middle. It starts with single bytes and then merges the most common pairs again and again. Common words like the, and, to and token quickly become single tokens. Rare words are split into smaller pieces that the model has seen before. This is why language models can read words they have never seen, and why they sometimes struggle to count the letters in a word: they never see the letters, only the tokens. The tokenizer is trained once, before the model, and then it is frozen. A model and its tokenizer must always be used together. Training a tokenizer on code makes it better at code, and training it on many languages makes it fairer to those languages. The number of tokens in your prompt decides how much you pay for an API call and how much text fits in the context window. Understanding the tokenizer helps you understand the model.
